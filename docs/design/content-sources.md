# CW content sources

Encoding follows [ITU-R M.1677-1](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-M.1677-1-200910-I).
Supported continuous procedural tokens are `<AR>` (end of message), `<AS>`
(wait), `<BT>` (separator), `<SK>` (end of contact), and `<HH>` (correction).
Ordinary letters such as `AR` retain their inter-character gap.

Operating usage is informed by [ARRL operating aids](https://www.arrl.org/quick-reference-operating-aids)
and [making your first contact](https://www.arrl.org/making-your-first-contact).
These are training examples, not a prescription for every operator or net.

Reviewed 2026-10-16. [ARRL operating aids](https://www.arrl.org/operating-aids)
and its [Q-signal reference](https://ema.arrl.org/q-signals-and-other-shortcuts/)
provide the vocabulary checks for reports, location and slower sending.
The original practice exchanges use K for an invitation, continuous AR for the
initial answer, BT between topics, and SK before the final callsign pair.
They include reports, name/location, repeats, QRS and sign-off, plus an eight-line
contact combining them. All content is editable.

W1AW is a real callsign used here solely as a consistent example counterpart.
Names, locations and reports in these exchanges are invented practice details,
not assertions about an actual contact, operator, or the learner's location.
There is no radio transmission. Separate station tones are a learning aid.

## NATO/ITU phonetic list

Verified A–Z against [ARRL Quick Reference Operating Aids](https://www.arrl.org/quick-reference-operating-aids)
on 2026-10-17, including Alfa, Juliett and X-ray. The individual letter is the
CW content; the spelling word is an explicit editable spoken override. Existing
callsign speech is unchanged. The same mapping supplies the terminal reference.
